Ask five studios for a price on the same website and you will get quotes between
£4,000 and £60,000. That range is not dishonesty. It is that "a website" describes
five different products.

## What you are actually buying

The build is rarely the expensive part. In a typical project the split is closer to:

- **Deciding what it should be** — 25%. Information architecture, content model,
  the argument the page makes.
- **Designing it** — 30%. Including the states nobody asks for and everybody needs.
- **Building it** — 30%.
- **The last 10%** — 15%. Accessibility, performance, the error states, the real
  content replacing the placeholder content.

That last bucket is where cheap projects are cheap. It is invisible on a screenshot
and it is the difference between a site that works and a site that demos.

## Why the range is real

A £4,000 site is a template with your content in it, built in a week. That is a
legitimate product and sometimes the right one. A £40,000 site is a bespoke design
system, a content model your team can actually operate, and a performance budget
somebody enforced. Both are "a website".

The question to ask a studio is not "how much". It is "what is in the last 10%".
